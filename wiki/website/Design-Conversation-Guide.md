# Design Conversation Guide

This note is meant to guide the design process step by step. Instead of forcing one style too quickly, the portfolio should be shaped through a conversation about taste, mood, and experience.

## Goal
Create a portfolio that feels:
- cinematic
- personal
- immersive
- modern
- emotionally resonant
- spatial and dimensional

## Current design direction
The portfolio should explore a 3D spatial feeling. This means the experience should feel layered, dimensional, and alive rather than flat.

## Confirmed preferences
- Mood: artistic feeling
- Palette: warmer antique tone
- Motion style: dramatic
- Cursor interaction: yes, the background should respond to the cursor

## Website content rule
- Do not use emoji glyphs in the website UI.
- Use text-only labels, simple shapes, or CSS styling instead.
- Keep the interface elegant, readable, and consistent.

## How we will build it
We will move step by step and ask questions at each stage so the final design reflects your taste rather than generic trends.

## Step 1 — Mood and atmosphere
We should decide the emotional tone of the website.

Questions to answer:
- Do you want the site to feel luxurious, mysterious, artistic, futuristic, or intellectual?
- Should it feel more like a gallery, a film, a product launch, or a personal brand experience?
- Do you want it to feel warm and human, or cold and high-tech?

## Step 2 — Visual identity
We should define the visual language.

Questions to answer:
- Do you prefer a dark moody palette, a warm antique palette, or a bright editorial palette?
- Do you want gold, bronze, ivory, deep burgundy, midnight blue, or something else?
- Should the website feel more Renaissance-inspired, modern minimalist, or cinematic noir?

## Step 3 — 3D spatial feeling
We should define what “spatial” means for this portfolio.

Questions to answer:
- Do you want subtle depth, like layered lighting and shadows?
- Do you want stronger depth, like floating panels, parallax, or sculptural motion?
- Do you want the experience to feel like a room, a stage, a gallery, or a digital installation?

## Step 4 — Motion style
We should decide how movement should feel.

Questions to answer:
- Should motion be elegant and subtle, or bold and dramatic?
- Do you want the page to gently breathe, or to feel like it unfolds in a cinematic sequence?
- Should the cursor affect the atmosphere, or should motion be more scroll-driven?

## Step 5 — Content experience
We should shape how the content is presented.

Questions to answer:
- Do you want your biography to feel poetic or direct?
- Do you want your projects to feel like case studies or visual showcases?
- Should the site feel more editorial, like a magazine, or more architectural, like a curated gallery?

## Step 6 — Interaction and personality
We should decide how the site should behave.

Questions to answer:
- Do you want the background to respond to the cursor?
- Do you want floating elements, light bloom, or subtle object movement?
- Do you want the interface to feel alive but restrained, or immersive and theatrical?

## Step 7 — Final refinement
We should review the site as a full experience.

Questions to answer:
- Does it feel personal enough?
- Does it feel premium enough?
- Does it feel like you?
- Does the 3D spatial idea come through clearly?

## Research direction
We should research references that fit the direction of:
- cinematic portfolio design
- immersive 3D-inspired web experiences
- Renaissance-inspired palettes and atmosphere
- editorial motion and layered depth
- spatial storytelling on the web

## Suggested research categories
- Immersive portfolio websites
- 3D-inspired web design
- Renaissance and baroque visual language
- Layered depth and ambient lighting
- Editorial motion systems
- Interactive cursor-based experiences

## Working principle
The portfolio should not feel like a generic template. It should feel like a crafted world designed around your personality, your identity, and your taste.
